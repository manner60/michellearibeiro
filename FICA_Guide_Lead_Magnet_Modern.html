SECTION 1: The FICA Tip Credit — Your Best-Kept Secret

Let's be honest: payroll taxes suck. You pay them every month, they eat into your margins, and you never see that money again. Right?

Wrong.

There's a federal tax credit specifically designed for restaurants, bars, and hospitality businesses — and most owners have never heard of it. It's called the FICA Tip Credit (IRS Section 45B), and it could put tens of thousands of dollars back in your pocket.

Here's the deal: Every time you pay FICA taxes on your employees' tips, you're overpaying. The government knows this, which is why they created a credit to give you that money back. You just have to claim it.

How It Actually Works

The IRS uses a "frozen" minimum wage of $5.15 per hour for this calculation. Any FICA taxes you pay on tips above that threshold? That's money you can recover. Not a deduction — a direct credit against what you owe.

The Bottom Line

A $10,000 deduction saves you maybe $2,500 in taxes.

A $10,000 tax credit saves you $10,000.

That's the difference between a deduction and a credit. This is real money back in your business.

SECTION 2: Why Your CPA Never Mentioned This

You're probably thinking: "If this is so great, why didn't my accountant tell me about it?"

Fair question. Here's the truth:

Your CPA Is a Generalist

Most accountants handle everything — contractors, retail shops, doctors, tech startups. They're focused on getting your return filed correctly, not hunting for specialty credits. The FICA Tip Credit requires digging into every single pay period, every employee, every week. That's not something most firms have time for.

The Data Is a Nightmare

To calculate this properly, someone needs to analyze every employee's hours and tips for the entire year. For a restaurant with 30 staff, that's over 1,500 data points. Most accounting firms don't have the software (or the patience) to do this manually. So they take the easy route and skip it entirely.

They're Playing It Safe

Some accountants avoid this credit because they're not sure how it interacts with other credits like the ERC. Rather than figure it out, they ignore it. That "safety" costs you thousands every single year.

SECTION 4: Do You Qualify? (3-Minute Checklist)

Most hospitality businesses qualify. Run through this quick checklist:

✓ Do You Have Tipped Employees?

Servers, bartenders, barbacks, bussers, delivery drivers, valets — if they get tips, they count.

✓ Are You Paying FICA Taxes on Tips?

If you use ADP, Gusto, Paychex, or any payroll service and report tips on your 941s, you're already doing this.

✓ Do You Pay a Tipped Wage?

If your staff earns $2.13, $5.00, or any tipped wage plus tips, you qualify. Even if you pay full minimum wage, you can claim credits on tips above the $5.15/hour threshold.

Check all three boxes? You're probably sitting on significant recoverable money.

SECTION 5: How to Actually Get Your Money Back

So how do you claim this? Two steps:

File Form 8846

This is the IRS form for claiming credits on tipped wages. For retroactive claims, you file an amended return for each year you missed.

The Look-Back Audit

Here's what needs to happen:

  1. Gather 36 months of payroll records
  2. Match every employee's tips to their hours worked
  3. Apply the $5.15/hour formula to every pay period
  4. Reconcile with your filed 941s and tax returns

Once that's done, file the amended forms. The IRS typically issues refunds as a check or credit toward future taxes.

SECTION 6: Don't Mess This Up — Common Mistakes

The IRS expects perfection on high-value credits. Here are the three biggest mistakes to avoid:

1. The ERC Overlap

If you claimed the Employee Retention Credit during COVID, you can't double-dip. The same wages can't count for both credits. Many DIY filers get this wrong and end up owing money back.
2. Wrong Minimum Wage Math

The credit only applies to tips above the $5.15/hour floor. Get this wrong and your claim gets rejected. This is the #1 reason the IRS denies these credits.
3. Forgetting the Accounting Adjustment

When you get this credit, you have to reduce your "Payroll Tax Expense" on your books. You can't deduct the tax AND claim the credit. Many owners miss this step.
